## Scope and acceptance

The owned Soul tool is included in both editions. It performs purchases, upgrades,
placement and consecutive dungeon progression with pause/resume, actual account
upgrade limits and retries of exhausted dungeons. Planning considers floors, units,
equipment and available coins together. Purchases and upgrades are visible in the
game. Simulated win rates are estimates and do not guarantee clearing a run.

Daily home navigation now handles Soul preparation, results and lobby pages through
their native exit paths. Preparation preserves progress, active battles wait for
settlement, and an underlying menu is not mistaken for completed navigation while
the Soul page remains visible.

Storage relocation includes the owned account sandbox stores as well as caches,
with verified copying, binding updates, rollback and interruption recovery. Stable
account records, control ownership and update recovery journals retain their
existing data boundaries. Storage usage is reported by drive.

Full local synthetic validation passed **7,547 checks in 135 groups**. The native
storage migration fixture was explicitly enabled and passed using a temporary
empty test sandbox and synthetic registry/file data, without touching real accounts.
The final publication workflow independently passed the same 7,547 checks.

Packaging uncovered a parallel publish collision in the shared connection apphost;
publish is now serialized. Missing Soul menu text was added in all three languages.
The Soul shared-host language/control probes and a tool-order-dependent UI assertion
were corrected. Focused UI validation passed **153 checks**, and both final editions
passed their complete startup, resource, integrated-tool and language/theme gates,
including Soul client compilation and six Soul smoke images per edition. The
failed packaging attempts and their evidence remain separate from the final build.

The original released **1.5.10** updater passed **10 cases** across both editions:
upgrade, failed-target startup rollback, interrupted replacement recovery via the
host and preserved helper, and damaged-base refusal without installation writes.
Exact files and account/plugin sentinels were checked. Interruption was injected;
this was not a physical power-cut test. Release acceptance did not connect to or
operate the game, and is not a claim of a fresh five-dungeon gameplay run on each
final archive. Earlier gameplay research remains separate evidence.
